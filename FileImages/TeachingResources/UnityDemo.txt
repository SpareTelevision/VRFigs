2-Minute Unity Environment Demo

Welcome to Unity! Unity is a game engine used to create 2D games, 3D
games, VR, AR, and even simulations. Today, I just want to show you the
main parts of the editor so you know where everything is.

Scene View

The largest window here is the Scene View. Think of this as your
workspace. You can click, drag, and move objects around to build your
game world. This is where you’ll spend most of your time designing
levels.

Game View

Next is the Game View. This shows what the player actually sees when
the game is running. You’ll switch between Scene and Game View
frequently while developing.

Hierarchy

On the left is the Hierarchy. Every object in your current scene is
listed here—things like cameras, lights, characters, and platforms. If
it’s in your level, you’ll find it here.

Inspector

On the right is the Inspector. Whenever I select an object, the
Inspector lets me edit its properties, like its position, rotation,
scale, or add components that give it new behaviors.

Project Window

At the bottom is the Project Window. This contains all of your assets:
scripts, sprites, models, sounds, animations, and materials. Think of it
as your project’s file explorer.

Toolbar

Across the top is the Toolbar. The most important button here is the
Play button. Pressing Play lets you test your game instantly, and
pressing it again stops the simulation.

Wrap-Up

That’s really all you need to get started. The Scene is where you
build, the Game View is what the player sees, the Hierarchy keeps track
of your objects, the Inspector edits them, and the Project Window stores
your assets. Once you’re comfortable with these five areas, you’re ready
to start creating games.

Demo Actions

1.  Click a cube in the Hierarchy.
2.  Show its Transform values in the Inspector.
3.  Move it in the Scene View.
4.  Switch to the Game View.
5.  Press Play for a few seconds, then stop.
6.  Drag an asset from the Project Window into the Scene.
